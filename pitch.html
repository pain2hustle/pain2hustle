Multi-Agent Orchestration Fleet

A 9-lane fleet (6 cloud + 3 local LLM lanes) with a conductor that partitions work so agents can't collide, free-first control flow (cloud escalates only after local retries fail), a “retry-till-real” quality gate so it never ships a fake result, and a self-refilling, deduped queue for 24/7 autonomy. Runs $0.

↳

Roadscape

A corridor-safety screening engine: public crash + traffic data + AASHTO geometry → evidence packets, a real-scale collision-free 3D traffic sim, and an agent that ranks real cities in need. Honest guardrails throughout — screening-grade, never PE-stamped; measured vs. estimated always labeled.

↳

NeighborDoors

A multi-source search + investigation engine with cross-source corroboration and cited AI briefs. Migrated onto Cloudflare Workers; AI runs on a free-model pool ($0).

↳

Apiary

An agentic, accessibility-first world for the tech-isolated — playable bee-world games, a real community + live feed, and a self-learning article fleet that teaches itself across topics (superposition, coding, conservation…) and compounds on its own past insights. The AI doesn't just serve content — it makes it.

↳

Service Pricer

Rescued a 144-route Next.js app off a capped host onto Cloudflare Workers — locked payment code untouched, squeezed under a hard size cap, no regression.

How I work

Free-first, in-house

Everything runs $0 on my own rotating pool of free + local models. I engineer around the constraint instead of buying past it.

Honest guardrails, always

Screening-grade is never sold as certified. Accuracy is a measured band, never a marketed number. I don't fake results — it's in the code.

Ship and iterate

Short scope, ship a chunk, watch real behavior, steer. A working thing to react to beats a frozen spec.

Translate to plain English

The hypothesis I'm chasing

“A system in superposition becomes legible when you can model the thing that collapses it.”

Strip the physics and there's a testable engineering claim underneath — and it's exactly what I built into my fleets: the chaos didn't get readable by adding agents, it got readable by building the collapse mechanism on purpose. I hold the speculative parts loosely and write them down honestly, weak spots included. The proof of real curiosity isn't certainty — it's the willingness to be wrong out loud.
